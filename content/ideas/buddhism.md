**The teaching.** The Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta, traditionally the Buddha's first sermon, delivered at Isipatana near Varanasi, identifies the origin of suffering as "the craving that makes for further becoming" and its cessation as "the remainderless fading & cessation, renunciation, relinquishment, release, & letting go of that very craving" [@sn-56-11].

**The dating problem.** The Theravada tradition dates the Buddha's death to 544/543 BCE. Checking it against the reign of the emperor Ashoka produced a corrected date of 487/486 BCE, while Indian sources point to 370–368 BCE. At a 1988 symposium on the question in Göttingen, experts proposed dates ranging from 486 to 261 BCE [@violatti-2015]. Reviewing the published volumes, Cousins concluded that "the evidence seems to favour some kind of median chronology and we should no doubt speak of a date for the Buddha's Mahaparinibbana of c.400 B.C." [@cousins-1996].
