## Entry-level checks

- **Summary and title:** The title is fine. The summary states as historical fact that the Buddha taught the Four Noble Truths in his first sermon. The body hedges this ('traditionally'), no cited source says 'first sermon', and the cited translation's note 1 reports scholars who argue the four truths are a later addition. The card overstates the body and the sources.
- **Date:** A year of -400 is well grounded in Cousins. Earliest -486 and latest -368 span the corrected long and short chronologies, but leave out the symposium's 261 BCE lower bound, which the body cites. Violatti's own conclusion is 410–370 BCE. The marks clause about the teaching career has no source. The note is accurate.
- **Attribution and framing:** Treating the Buddha as founder is standard. The main framing problem is presenting the canonical account of the first sermon and its content as history. The entry should say this is how the tradition and the sutta present it, and acknowledge the debate about whether the four-truths formula is early.
- **Links:** No buildsOn links. Arguably the entry should link to earlier Indian śramaṇa or Upaniṣadic ideas, but that is a suggestion, not a requirement.
- **Searches for disagreement:**
  - "four noble truths later addition first sermon Norman Schmithausen Bronkhorst": The cited translation's own note 1 states that some scholars consider 'noble truth', and even the content of the four truths, later additions. Search leads point to Schmithausen, Bronkhorst, Norman and Anderson (1999) as proponents. The summary ignores this debate.
  - "Lumbini excavation Coningham 6th century BCE Buddha date Antiquity 2013": Coningham et al. (Antiquity 2013) argued from a timber structure at Lumbini for a sixth-century BCE Buddha. The interpretation is contested, and the entry does not mention it. Non-blocking, since the entry already presents the range of chronologies, but worth a sentence.
  - "Cousins "median chronology" Buddha "c.400 B.C."": Confirmed Cousins's wording through Choong (Journal of Indian History) and Wujastyk's INDOLOGY reproduction. Choong lists other scholarly proposals: Bechert 448–368, Gombrich 502/479–422/399, Bareau 500/460–420/380. These agree broadly with a 5th–4th-century death.

## Blocking issues

1. Summary: reword so the first-sermon setting and the Four Noble Truths are attributed to the tradition or the canonical account (e.g. 'In what tradition holds was his first sermon…'), not stated as historical fact. The cited translation's note 1 reports scholars who argue the four truths are a later addition.
2. Body: 'traditionally the Buddha's first sermon' needs a source that actually says so. SN 56.11 does not call itself the first sermon, and neither Violatti nor Cousins says it.
3. Origin: 'Siddhartha Gautama' and 'northern India' appear in no cited source. Add a reference that gives the name and region, or drop them.
4. Date marks: 'his teaching career came in the decades before' has no source. Add one (e.g. for the traditional ~45-year teaching career or the death at about eighty, with the inference made explicit), or delete the clause.

## Non-blocking suggestions

- Fix the cousins-1996 note: the 1997 web text is Dominik Wujastyk's HTML reproduction on the INDOLOGY website (ucl.ac.uk/~ucgadkw/position/buddha/), not 'the author's own post to the INDOLOGY list'. Consider citing that URL directly so readers can check the passage.
- Consider widening 'latest' or explaining it in the note: the Göttingen proposals reached 261 BCE, and Violatti concludes 410–370 BCE.
- Mention that Thanissaro translates dukkha as 'stress', since the entry puts 'suffering' next to quotations that, in the original, define 'stress'.
- Consider one sentence on the 2013 Lumbini excavation claim (Coningham et al., Antiquity) for a sixth-century BCE Buddha, and noting that it is contested.
- Note that Violatti's piece was first published on Ancient.eu (Ancient History Encyclopedia) in 2015 and republished on OpenLearn.
